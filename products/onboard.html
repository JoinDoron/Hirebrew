Why OnboardCapabilitiesUse casesHow it connectsQuestions

A signed offer is not a starting plan.

The offer is accepted, but the manager is busy, introductions are informal and the first week has no clear purpose. New employees assemble their own welcome. Onboard connects practical tasks with human ones, giving each starter a clearer path from preparation to their first useful contribution.

Prepare before the first morning

Bring preboarding tasks and owners into view while there is still time to act. Separate what HR must prepare from what the manager and new starter need to contribute.

Give the manager a visible part

A welcome is more than an HR checklist. Include introductions, role expectations and first-week conversations in the manager handoff, with responsibility for follow-through.

Connect a good start to progress

Use the first 30, 60 and 90 days to clarify priorities and learning. Help the employee understand what comes next instead of treating orientation as the finish line.

Practical ways to evaluate Onboard with your team. These are workflow examples, not customer case studies.

A first employee in a new location+

The situationA growing business welcomes a team member outside its Lagos headquarters.

With HirebrewAgree on the local manager, essential introductions and location-specific policy questions before the employee starts.

What changesA welcome with local ownership rather than a headquarters checklist that leaves practical questions unanswered.

Several starters joining together+

The situationHR coordinates a group of new employees while managers have different team priorities.

With HirebrewSeparate shared preparation from each manager's first-week plan, then review the responsibilities for every new starter.

What changesA consistent foundation without assuming that every person needs the same learning or introductions.

A technical hire learning the business+

The situationA specialist understands their discipline but is new to the organisation, its customers and its working practices.

With HirebrewCombine practical onboarding tasks with manager conversations and a 30/60/90-day outline for learning the business context.

What changesEarly expectations that include understanding and relationships, not only a list of completed tasks.

Good questions.
Clear answers.

Is onboarding only an HR responsibility?+

No. HR, the manager and the employee each contribute. Agree on responsibilities during the pilot so handoffs do not depend on informal follow-up.

Does Hirebrew provision laptops and accounts?+

No automatic provisioning is demonstrated. Plan those dependencies with your IT and equipment owners, and verify any future connection separately.

What happens after the first month?+

Use the 30/60/90-day plan to continue the conversation, then explore goals, check-ins and learning in Grow. The first month should establish a rhythm rather than close the employee journey.
