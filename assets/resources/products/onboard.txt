HIREBREW ONBOARD | PRODUCT PILOT BRIEF

Make the first day a real beginning.
Give new starters, managers and HR a shared welcome plan that continues beyond paperwork into confidence, connection and early progress.

CAPABILITIES TO EXPLORE
Preboarding and new-joiner tasks
Organise the practical steps that prepare a new starter for day one, with a clear view of what remains to be done.

Manager and HR handoffs
Make shared responsibilities explicit so a task is not considered complete simply because it left the recruiter's desk.

A first-week starting point
Give the employee a task-led welcome, with introductions, context and realistic expectations from their manager.

30/60/90-day planning
Carry the welcome into early goals, learning and check-ins, connecting initial expectations with an ongoing development conversation.

PRACTICAL USE CASES
A first employee in a new location
Situation: A growing business welcomes a team member outside its Lagos headquarters.
Workflow: Agree on the local manager, essential introductions and location-specific policy questions before the employee starts.
Intended improvement: A welcome with local ownership rather than a headquarters checklist that leaves practical questions unanswered.

Several starters joining together
Situation: HR coordinates a group of new employees while managers have different team priorities.
Workflow: Separate shared preparation from each manager's first-week plan, then review the responsibilities for every new starter.
Intended improvement: A consistent foundation without assuming that every person needs the same learning or introductions.

A technical hire learning the business
Situation: A specialist understands their discipline but is new to the organisation, its customers and its working practices.
Workflow: Combine practical onboarding tasks with manager conversations and a 30/60/90-day outline for learning the business context.
Intended improvement: Early expectations that include understanding and relationships, not only a list of completed tasks.

WORKFLOW
From ready to join to ready to contribute.
Prepare | HR + hiring manager
Turn the handoff into a plan
Agree on the start context, manager and first-week priorities. Assign preboarding responsibilities before the new employee arrives, including dependencies owned outside the people team.

Welcome | New starter + HR
Make the essentials understandable
Give the employee a clear task list and a place to begin. Explain what each task supports and who can help when something is missing or unclear.

Connect | Manager + team
Make room for the human welcome
Plan introductions, initial learning and the first manager conversation. Check that the employee knows the team's priorities and how to ask for support.

Progress | Employee + manager
Look beyond the first week
Review a 30/60/90-day outline together. Adjust goals as the employee learns, identify development needs and make the next check-in a deliberate part of the journey.

PILOT
Walk through one new starter's first month.
Use a fictional employee and ask HR, a manager and a colleague to complete their part. Include a missing dependency, not only a perfect start.
