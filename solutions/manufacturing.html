Production has a plan. People handoffs need one too.

A replacement request starts at a plant. Approval happens at head office. Interviews involve a technical supervisor, while a different team prepares the new starter. When each handoff lives in its own conversation, nobody sees the whole journey. Hirebrew brings these people workflows into one product direction, with named responsibilities from requisition to the first manager check-in.

Make site demand visible

Capture the role, location and hiring rationale before recruitment begins. Give site leaders and HR a shared starting point for discussing vacancies instead of reconciling separate lists at the end of the week.

Keep technical hiring structured

Bring supervisors into interview panels and use scorecards to record role-relevant evidence. Keep candidate questions in the hiring conversation so practical details do not disappear between central recruitment and the site.

Carry ownership past the offer

Assign onboarding tasks to the people who can complete them. Follow the welcome with employee requests, learning conversations and manager check-ins, connecting plant and office employees to the same people-service approach.

From a site vacancy to a supported starter.

A few good questions.

Can we use different onboarding tasks by site?+

Location-specific responsibilities are a useful pilot scenario for Hirebrew's task-based onboarding. Test the setup with your actual operating model before making a multi-site rollout commitment.

Does Hirebrew replace our plant systems?+

No. The focus is hiring and employee coordination. Production planning, clocking, shift scheduling and safety systems remain specialist tools; any required connection must be scoped separately.

What should a Nigerian manufacturing pilot include?+

Include both frontline and office participants, realistic connectivity conditions and the languages used for employee support. Validate those access needs rather than assuming a desktop-first process will suit every site.
