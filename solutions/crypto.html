Your roadmap moves fast. Hiring needs a memory.

Protocol engineers, product operators and community specialists bring very different strengths. When interview notes live in separate chats, decisions lose their context. Hirebrew brings the hiring conversation into a clearer journey, so a promising candidate does not disappear between technical review, business approval and their first week.

Judge the role, not the hype

Agree on role-specific criteria before interviews. Use panel scorecards to separate demonstrated engineering, operational or communication skills from familiarity with a particular ecosystem.

Keep distributed candidates informed

Explore two-way candidate conversations alongside pipeline stages. Assign a response owner instead of assuming someone in another location has already followed up.

Make a fast start a considered start

Give each new hire a manager, a first-week task list and clear early expectations. Keep employment and jurisdiction questions with the appropriate advisers.

Workflow ownerHiring manager + HR

Define what the role must deliver

Create the hiring request around the team's actual need. Agree on the approver, interview criteria and who can make the final selection before candidates enter the pipeline.

Workflow ownerTechnical + business panel

Bring different perspectives together

Collect structured feedback from the people assessing engineering depth, collaboration and operational judgement. Review evidence against the same role requirements without turning one score into an automatic decision.

Workflow ownerRecruiter + candidate

Keep the conversation open

Use the candidate messaging journey to explain the next step and receive questions. Agree on local working hours for an African and internationally distributed team.

Workflow ownerManager + new hire

Turn selection into a useful start

Explore onboarding tasks and a 30/60/90-day plan. Name the person responsible for each handoff, while access provisioning remains with your existing technology owners.

A few good questions.

Can we evaluate a distributed team?+

Yes. Map participants, locations and working hours in the pilot. Test access and notification requirements separately before considering live use.

Does Hirebrew manage tokens or worker classification?+

No. Token compensation, payments, worker classification and jurisdiction-specific employment requirements remain with your existing systems and qualified advisers.

Is Brew making hiring decisions?+

No. Brew examples are illustrative drafts. Live AI requires separate implementation and safeguards, and people remain accountable for selection.
