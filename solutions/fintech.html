A new hire is a cross-functional decision.

A payments business needs people who can collaborate across product ambition and operational responsibility. Recruitment can stall when feedback, approvals and onboarding requirements sit with different teams. Hirebrew helps you explore a shared people journey, with explicit owners instead of an assumption that the next department has everything it needs.

Make selection evidence visible

Define what good looks like for an engineer, risk operations analyst or support lead. Bring the relevant panel together around structured, role-specific feedback.

Treat onboarding as a handoff

Connect HR and the manager around first-week responsibilities. Record the tasks that need specialist review without implying that a checked box establishes regulatory readiness.

Ask governance questions early

Map candidate and employee information before a pilot. Include your privacy and technology owners, especially where teams work across Nigerian and other African markets.

Compare evidence, not impressions

Assign first-week responsibilities

Workflow ownerManager + employee

Continue the conversation after day one

Use goals and check-ins to review the employee's first month. Turn learning needs into a development discussion rather than leaving them in the original interview notes.

A few good questions.

Does Hirebrew replace our compliance systems?+

No. It focuses on people workflows. Financial-services controls, screening and regulatory obligations remain with your specialist systems and advisers.

Can it connect to our existing HRIS?+

Treat that as an implementation workstream. Map fields, owners and reconciliation tests; the website does not demonstrate a live HRIS connection.

What should our first pilot measure?+

Use your baseline for unresolved handoffs, response times and onboarding completion. Agree on targets yourselves rather than relying on unverified performance claims.
