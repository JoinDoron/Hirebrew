Growth exposes the gaps between departments.

One department hires through email. Another keeps its own tracker. Employees ask different people the same leave question, and managers approach reviews differently. At this stage, consistency matters, but forcing every team into an identical process can create more work. Hirebrew helps you evaluate a shared foundation with explicit owners and considered local exceptions.

Standardise the useful parts

Create a common hiring journey around requisitions, stages and structured feedback. Agree which decisions belong to central HR and which stay with department managers.

Give employees a clearer front door

Explore leave, letters and employee requests in a consistent experience. Make the next responsible person easier to identify when a request crosses teams.

Keep development in the operating rhythm

Bring goals, check-ins and learning into manager conversations. Evaluate how development continues after onboarding, not only during an annual review.

A shared process. Ownership close to the work.

Evaluate one employee journey across two departments. Their differences will reveal where you need flexibility and where inconsistency adds no value.

A few good questions.

Must every department work identically?+

No. Begin by agreeing shared stages and responsibilities, then identify necessary differences. Configuration requirements should be captured and validated during the pilot, not assumed.

Can we keep our existing payroll system?+

That is the sensible evaluation starting point. Define required employee-data exchanges with its owner; a live connection is subject to separate implementation and testing.

How should we measure value?+

Record your own starting point for stalled handoffs, request completion and manager participation. Compare a limited pilot with that baseline rather than relying on promised savings.
