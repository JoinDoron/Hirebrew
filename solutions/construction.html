Every new project creates a people handoff.

The project team knows the role it needs. HR runs the search. A site manager makes time for interviews, and another colleague prepares the welcome. Across changing locations and project phases, that chain can become a series of follow-ups. Hirebrew gives you a place to explore a more deliberate path from a vacancy to an employee who knows their manager and next steps.

Make the hiring rationale clear

Frame each requisition around the role and project need before recruitment starts. Keep the approval conversation connected to the vacancy so HR and operational leaders work from the same brief.

Bring site expertise into interviews

Invite the people who understand the work to contribute structured scorecards. Keep practical candidate questions visible through two-way communication instead of leaving HR to relay every answer manually.

Name the owner of each arrival task

Use onboarding tasks for introductions, role expectations and early manager contact. Make the administrative handoff explicit while leaving site access, safety clearance and specialist competency approval in their established processes.

A few good questions.

Does Hirebrew manage construction projects?+

No. The focus is the employee journey around a project: recruitment, onboarding, requests and development. Programme planning, quantities, procurement and project accounting remain in specialist tools.

Can we use it for mandatory site clearance?+

Do not treat the preview as a site-clearance system. Qualified owners must retain safety and competency decisions in approved processes. Hirebrew can be evaluated for the surrounding administrative handoffs.

How can a multi-site contractor evaluate it?+

Choose one project and one role family, involve both site and head-office participants, and test the actual access conditions. Establish responsibilities and production requirements before extending the workflow.
