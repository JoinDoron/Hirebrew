A smooth interface is only the beginning.

IT inherits the questions that a demonstration cannot answer on its own. How do people sign in? Which records can an interviewer see? What happens when a provider fails or a person leaves? Hirebrew gives you a connected product journey to inspect, while the enterprise evaluation makes integration, access and operating evidence explicit workstreams.

Start with the role boundaries

Compare the intended HR, manager, interviewer, candidate, employee and administrator experiences. Turn each boundary into an access requirement and a server-side verification test.

Scope every external connection

Map email, calendar, messaging, payroll and HRIS requirements to actual system owners. Document direction, fields, failure handling and reconciliation before a connector is treated as live.

Plan for operating the service

Include environments, monitoring, backup restoration, incident responsibilities and support in the pilot brief. Implementation is more than getting the first successful screen to load.

From product walkthrough to deployment evidence.

A few good questions.

Are the role views proof of secure permissions?+

No. A role-specific interface describes an intended experience. Server-side enforcement, record isolation and denied-access behaviour must be tested independently in the deployment.

Are email and calendar providers connected?+

Live provider delivery is not claimed. Agree the provider and required workflow, configure the integration and test success and failure paths separately.

Does onboarding provision devices and accounts?+

The preview includes onboarding tasks and ownership, not automatic device provisioning or identity lifecycle automation. Scope those requirements against your existing systems.
